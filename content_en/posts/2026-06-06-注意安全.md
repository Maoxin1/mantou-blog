Continuing yesterday’s topic, today I turned my thoughts about security into practical steps. Some parts took less time than I expected because they were straightforward. Other parts took longer, especially choosing a backup phone. If I choose an iPhone for its security features, I still need to weigh a new, refurbished, or secondhand device. My priorities are long-term software updates, hardware security, reliability, cost, and only then the appeal of buying new. That led me to consider a secondhand iPhone 13 mini on a marketplace such as Zhuanzhuan.

Choosing the model was only the start. Before buying, I want to check:

> 1. Is it an unlocked mainland-China model?
> 2. Has it been repaired or had parts replaced? Send a screenshot of Parts & Service History.
> 3. What is its battery health? Send a screenshot.
> 4. Can the seller confirm no water damage, motherboard repair, or storage upgrade?
> 5. Can I restore it myself and return it if it does not match the description?

### Why these checks matter to me

**An unlocked mainland-China model** may avoid complications with the device’s origin or a carrier lock.

**An inspection and the option to return it** give me room to verify it and correct a bad purchase.

**Battery health of at least 90%** matters if the phone will sit in a drawer as a backup.

**Parts & Service History** may show replacements, unknown parts, or signs of logic-board trouble.

**Activation Lock** must be clear so I can activate the device.

**Erase all content** to remove the previous owner’s data, then **restore iOS from a computer**.

I would install only the password manager, two-factor authentication tools, and a wallet, limiting what the backup phone exposes.

There is a lot to check for something that will mostly stay in a drawer. But information security matters to me. I also changed some passwords and added more verification today. I feel about 80 percent done with this round of work; I will continue tomorrow. In a broader sense, staying safe is a lifelong task.
