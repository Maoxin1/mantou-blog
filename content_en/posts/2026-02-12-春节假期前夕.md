It is my last day in Guangzhou before going home for New Year. I am still trying to find a useful plan for the short break. I do not want to leave it at “have a good rest”; I want time to think about the coming year. Yet the harder I press, the less clearly I see.

After lunch I did not know where to go, so I chose a quiet café and opened the Bible. Half an iced Americano later, I grew sleepy. I let myself nap.

![Coffee](/images/posts/2026-02-12-1.jpg)

When I woke, my thoughts felt renewed. Scrolling X, I could suddenly pick out useful posts from the noise. Then it struck me: I do not lack things to do. I have too many. I want to learn Git and Go, experiment further with Clawdbot until I make something real, understand the differences among Linux, Windows, and macOS, decide which system suits me, work out whether I can afford the AI tokens for more experiments, and solve how to pay for ChatGPT.

![A post on X](/images/posts/2026-02-12-2.jpg)

The list excites and saddens me. I still have the energy to try, but many earlier projects ended abruptly without a serious review. That leaves too many regrets I cannot explain or learn from.

![Experimenting](/images/posts/2026-02-12-3.jpg)

A crypto bot I began around last September ended only last week. It taught me some practical internet skills, but I had not thought the project through at the start. Halfway in, I found it of little use and stopped. That cost time I could have spent better. I want to bring more care and rigor to the next project.
