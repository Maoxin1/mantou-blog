I do not want to count finishing five books in their original English as completing the task. This checklist sets the standards for my learning plan: each book should leave me with something I can use, test, and review.

## Six questions for every piece of work

I will check each learning outcome against these six questions:

1. **Problem:** What real problem did I start with?
2. **Input:** What new model did the book give me?
3. **Application:** Where, specifically, did I apply it?
4. **Evidence:** What shows that it made a difference?
5. **Limitations:** What remains unresolved?
6. **Artifact:** What have I made that I can keep using?

I will update the checklist below as my reading progresses.

## Five English books → five pieces of work

### Overall aim

The goal is to turn five books into five real pieces of work, rather than simply write five sets of reading notes.

Each piece should:

* Address a real problem.
* Explain the book’s central model clearly.
* Apply that model at least once.
* Include supporting evidence or a case.
* Identify limitations or counterexamples.
* Leave a reusable result.
* Be documented as a project on the blog.

The result might be:

> An article / case study / checklist / standard operating procedure / README / prompt / template / tool / method.

**I will leave the form open, but set the acceptance criteria in advance.**

## 1. Beyond Feelings

The ability I want to develop:

> **Judge whether a claim is reliable.**

Requirements:

* Develop my own framework for judgment.
* Analyze one real claim from start to finish.
* Distinguish claim, evidence, reasoning, and assumption.
* Record how my judgment changes after reading.
* Identify the framework’s limits.

The central question:

> **How can I judge a claim more reliably?**

## 2. The Craft of Research

The ability I want to develop:

> **Complete a structured research project.**

Requirements:

* Pose one real research question.
* Record how I narrow it down.
* Organize sources, evidence, and counterevidence.
* Complete a research memo.
* Describe my research workflow.

The central question:

> **How can I carry out credible, in-depth research on my own?**

## 3. Competing Against Luck

The ability I want to develop:

> **Understand why people actually choose a product.**

Requirements:

* Choose one real product or behavior.
* Write down my original explanation for it.
* Reinterpret it using Jobs Theory.
* Analyze the situation, desired progress, hiring decision, and alternatives.
* Derive new product decisions.

The central question:

> **What job is the user really “hiring” this product to do?**

## 4. When Coffee and Kale Compete

The ability I want to develop:

> **Understand how a user actually switches products.**

Requirements:

* Choose one real switch.
* Reconstruct the full decision timeline.
* Analyze push, pull, anxiety, and habit.
* Find user motivations I had previously missed.
* Identify at least one new insight about users.

The central question:

> **How does a real product switch happen?**

## 5. The Jobs To Be Done Playbook

The ability I want to develop:

> **Carry out Jobs to Be Done (JTBD) research in practice.**

Requirements:

* Select the JTBD tools that are actually useful.
* Assemble my own toolkit.
* Choose one real project.
* Run the process from the initial research question onward.
* Complete interviews, evidence gathering, and analysis.
* Define the job and the opportunities.
* Make recommendations for a product or an action.
* Record which tools helped and which did not.

The central piece of work:

> **My practical JTBD workflow, V1.**

## The progression across five books

The intended result is more than:

> Having read five English books.

I want to develop the full sequence of abilities:

> **Judgment → research → modeling → insight → execution.**

## The check at the end of each book

I will copy this checklist for each book. The most important rule can sit at the top of every draft:

> **Completion means producing something I can verify, not simply reaching the last page.**
