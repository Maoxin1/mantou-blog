For a long time I have been drawn to the strongest players, though perhaps not in the usual sense of the phrase. In markets I look first at leaders: Starbucks in coffee, Tesla in electric vehicles, Nvidia in AI, and Bitcoin in digital assets. I see the Matthew effect at work: success can give a leader more advantages, and I think that effect may be growing.

I make similar choices in daily life, seeking out tools and products I regard as leaders. In investing, I prefer to study large, established companies. When a beginner asks me how to start, I usually offer two thoughts: give up the fantasy of getting rich overnight, and examine assets with deep global trading activity. Those are my preferences, not guarantees.

Recently I started looking at memory technology and focused on three major players: Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Weighing geopolitics and valuations as I understood them, I chose a large position in Micron ($MU). I bought it before I knew everything I wanted to know, partly because having real money at stake would force me to study honestly. That may be an uncomfortable method, but it has worked to hold my attention in the past.

After buying, I thought about the company almost every hour, sometimes even in dreams. Spending money without understanding the business would feel absurd. Then the share price fell for a sustained period and alarming stories spread. That pushed me to seek sources, verify claims, and learn more deeply. I also found a wealth of high-quality learning material on YouTube.

The paper loss began to feel less central than the question I set out to answer: can I build the ability to make sound decisions and earn sustainably over time? That matters more to me than a short-term price move.
